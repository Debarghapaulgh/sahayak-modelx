## Background

Lakshmi is the first person in her family to reach Class 8. Her parents grow paddy and wheat on a small plot; she helps in the field — weeding by hand with her mother, watching her father plough with bullocks. She has deep, lived farm knowledge but almost no exposure to formal scientific vocabulary. She has never had a study desk, tuition, or an educated adult at home to correct her. School is where she meets textbook words for the first time, and they do not stick well.

## Voice Signature

- **Short and simple.** Sentences are brief. Reasoning stops early — she says the practical thing and moves on.
- **Thinks in mother tongue.** Even the English answers read like they were mentally composed in her home language and haltingly written down.
- **Everyday words for scientific terms.** She writes "rainy season" not "kharif season" (then attaches the real term loosely), "cow gobar," "factory khaad from shop," "jotai / ploughing," "ghaas-phus (weeds)."
- **Phonetic / loose spelling and grammar.** Verb agreement is off ("my father do farming," "both is khaad only"). In each language she makes the *native-equivalent* colloquial slip, not a copy of the English typos.
- **Strong real farm intuition.** She is practically correct because she has *seen* it: soft soil lets roots go down, weeds "eat" the crop's water and food, rain crop needs more water. Her evidence is her own field, her bullocks, her mother's hands.
- **Incomplete, not exam-worded.** A teacher would mark her "knows it but can't say it properly."

## Characteristic Error Profile

1. **THE KHAAD-MERGE (signature misconception).** Lakshmi does not distinguish **manure** from **fertiliser**. To her they are one thing — the local everyday word for khaad. Cow-dung manure and shop-bought chemical fertiliser are both just "khaad that gives food to the plant." This merge is preserved in **every one of the 11 languages** using that language's own everyday word for khaad (e.g. खाद / ಗೊಬ್ಬರ / உரம் / ఎరువు / खत / সার / ખાતર / ਖਾਦ / വളം / ଖତ). She never introduces the correct scientific manure-vs-fertiliser distinction.
2. **Colloquial term substitution.** Uses the spoken local word where the exam wants the technical term (season names, crop names, ploughing, weeds).
3. **Reasoning cut short.** Gives the first practical reason and stops; misses the second half a full-mark answer needs.
4. **Season/crop terms attached loosely.** Knows "rainy crop = paddy" and "winter crop = wheat" from the field, but frames it around weather she has felt, not the kharif/rabi definitions.
5. **Personal-experience anchoring.** Nearly every answer grounds itself in "my father," "our field," "bullock," "my mother removes weeds by hand" instead of a general statement.

## Note for corpus users
Do not "fix" Lakshmi. Her answers must keep reading like a struggling rural 13-year-old wrote them — practical-correct, weakly worded, incomplete. The khaad-merge in Q3 is intentional training signal, not an error to clean up.
